Bangladesh

Our electricity grid is built on a backbone of 400 kV, 230 kV, and 132kV transmission lines connecting 140+ electricity generators to more than 50 million consumers.

Due to the practical differences between planning scenarios and realities of economic growth, underutilisation as well as congestion are observed at various points within our electricity transmission network. In this map, transmission line colour changes from green to yellow to red as the degree loading varies throughout the day - the dark red colour represents lines that are approaching overload limits. Future developers of power generation projects or large industrial complexes, or in other words, large users of the electricity grid can obtain cursory information from this map to determine suitable locations for their investment.

When it comes to loading of transmission lines, it is important to consider that AC electric power systems need reactive power, which is a different type of power than that we generally consume and pay for, to maintain necessary voltages at the consumption points. The flow of reactive power needs space in the electricity grid and therefore creates an additional burden on the electric lines. As a simple graphical example, see how the grid loading changes with and without reactive power flow. The map additionally shows voltages and power factors at all transmission substations. Power factor is an operational property of the grid that indicates some sort of ratio of reactive power to the real power, i.e., the power we pay for at a consumption point. If the power factor drops below a certain limit, the consumer will generally need to pay a penalty to the electricity supplier.

The power that is sold at the delivery and consumption points across the electricity network is generated by a fleet of generators owned publicly and privately. Generators typically dispatch their outputs at instructions issued by the National Load Dispatch Centre (NLDC) - an entity within Power Grid Bangladesh. Typically, the generators should be dispatched following an economic dispatch principle that determines the output of each generator in a way so that the total electricity production cost across the country is minimum. It is generally known that a full economic dispatch is yet to be exercised within the Bangladesh system. An empirical dispatch method is used which is developed based on years of data and experience and appears to provide a reasonable approximation of the theoretical economic dispatch output. Yet, a more methodical implementation of an economic dispatch approach might generate financial savings on fuel expenses, as this map shows. Some efforts in this regard might be beneficial for our national interest.

A number grid expansion projects are currently ongoing as identified on this map. Each project consists of additional network elements such as transmission lines and substations as well as augmentation of existing substations. The amount of additional electricity that could be provided to the grid-users can be determined using the models working behind these maps.

A future industrial complex or a solar project developer, or generally an infrastructure planner can find it useful to see the distance of the nearest grid substations from a given point on the map. A simple click on this map can make this happen considering the linear distance between the clicked point and the substations. In addition, it provides an indicative cost of the transmission line from the given point based on an average cost per km assumption.

Talk to Us

A piece of infrastructure on a geographic map generates insights, simulates ideas and creates constructive debates at a deeper level than what is possible by the notion of that infrastructure by itself. We aspire to build on this thought-provoking feature of ‘atlas’, that is, a collection of maps, by embedding useful information on our sustainable energy infrastructure on geographic maps to enhance public awareness on its sustainable development, use, and transformation.

Electrification of economic sectors is increasingly becoming a core approach to achieve energy transition goals. This inspired us to build the platform on electricity infrastructure as a foundational layer which will serve as the base container to explore our sustainable energy activities, intra- and inter-sectoral interactions among relevant economic sectors, and identify emerging economic opportunities.

This web-based platform is open to the public and currently maintained by efforts and resources volunteered by a group of professionals. Contribution and support are welcome to continue and advance the usefulness of this initiative. We are keen to hear your thoughts on potential collaboration. A few preconceived ideas to support includes:

  • #1. Volunteering your efforts to advance the initiative.
  • #2. Connecting us with interested public and private sector entities that could support this initiative through projects/activities.
  • #3. Spreading the news within your networks that we would be glad to collaborate!
